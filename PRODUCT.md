# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js (React) with static export (`output: "export"`), published on GitHub Pages. The user asked for a framework; Next was chosen over plain React because it is the same framework as the author's villagelab project and exports to static files Pages can serve. Inference runs in the browser with onnxruntime-web; there is no server.

## Users

- **Recruiters (served first):** reach the site from a resume or LinkedIn link, on phone or laptop, and give it about a minute. They need to grasp what was built and see it work without ML background.
- **ML hiring managers and engineers (served in depth):** judge whether the author handles data, leakage, evaluation and deployment carefully. They scroll, check numbers, try the live scanner and open the repo.
- The author also opens the live scanner on a phone during interviews and points it at real traffic.

## Product Purpose

indian-anpr reads Indian number plates: a fine-tuned YOLO26n plate detector, a small CRNN reader trained only on synthetic plates, and a plate-grammar corrector that fixes OCR confusions by character position. The site tells how it was built, story first, then lets the visitor run it live on their own camera. Success: a visitor leaves knowing it works on Indian plates, trusting the numbers, and seeing the author as someone who checks their data.

## Positioning

Most ANPR portfolio projects copy one YouTube tutorial (SORT tracker, EasyOCR, Western plate format) and report a detector score. This one is specific to Indian plates and measured end to end:
- plate grammar (state codes, BH series, Delhi one-digit districts, older plates without leading zeros) used to correct OCR and to flag bad dataset labels;
- leakage found and removed: over half the labelled images repeat a plate (split by plate number instead), and the 15k-image detection set is ~5k photos with Roboflow augmented copies spread across its own train/test, which explains its advertised 99%;
- the reader trains on synthetic plates only (licence-clean to ship) and is tested on real plates it never saw;
- runs entirely in the browser; camera video never leaves the device.

## Operating Context

- Reached from a resume, LinkedIn, or the GitHub repo's homepage link.
- Live scanner needs camera permission; on phones it uses the rear camera. Desk visitors often have no plates in view; there are deliberately no sample clips (user decision), so the page must be useful without a working camera.
- Model weights download to the browser on first use (detector ~10 MB, reader ~3 MB).
- Readers may compare claims with the repo README and `reports/`.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Numbers come only from pipeline outputs in `reports/` (e.g. `reports/baseline.json`). Nothing hand-typed or invented.
- Test set: Sai Sirisha N et al., Indian vehicle license plate dataset (Kaggle, CC BY-NC-ND): 431 images, held out by plate number. Its images must not be redistributed on the site.
- Live scanner: detect, read, correct, vote across frames per tracked vehicle; shows boxes over the video and a list of plates read with confidence and time. Nothing is uploaded or stored.
- Pending (models training on Kaggle at time of writing): fine-tuned detector score, synthetic-trained reader accuracy, final results table, ONNX weights, measured in-browser frame rate. The site must work with these absent and show them once they exist.

## Brand Commitments

- First-person voice, credited to Dushyant Rajpurohit, consistent with the paysim-fraud-detection story site.
- Honesty is the brand: limits are stated (OLX photos are close-up and easier than traffic; the 47-image free sample was too small; the test set was looked at once for error analysis before tuning rules moved to the train split).

## Evidence on Hand

- Baseline on the 431 test images (`reports/baseline.json`): old generic detector finds 96.3% of plates; EasyOCR reads 18.8% exactly (95% CI 15.4–22.8%); with the corrector 28.1% (24.0–32.5%); character error rate 0.36 to 0.34.
- Dataset audit: 1,697 labelled images, 74–80 bad labels (car names such as CRETA, typos such as RJD1AB1234, trade and temporary plates); 219 plate numbers repeat across 932 images.
- Synthetic plate renders (`reports/synth_preview.jpg`), generated by `anpr/synth.py`.
- Repo (local, not yet public): `Documents/indian-anpr`. Old tutorial repo: github.com/DushyantRajpurohit/Object-Detection-using-YOLOv8.
- Absent and not to be fabricated: real deployment, users, testimonials, any accuracy number not produced by the pipeline, traffic footage.

## Product Principles

1. Show it working before explaining it, but the story comes first on the page; the live scanner is the payoff.
2. Every number traces to a file in `reports/`.
3. Show the traps found in the data; they are the evidence of judgement.
4. Private by construction: the camera never leaves the device, and the page says so plainly.
5. Works without a camera, without plates in view, and before the models load.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No product-specific requirement established beyond a standard accessible page. Camera-dependent features need a clear non-camera path, and results need text equivalents.
